# Choosing your inventory source

Your **inventory source** is where new books come from. Bookhead enriches books
from that source and writes them to your Shopify store. Choosing a source is the
first decision you make after installing the app.

There are three sources, listed here in the order most bookstores use them.

## 1. A distributor file (recommended)

This is the primary, recommended way to bring in new titles. When you receive a
shipment from a distributor such as Ingram, you also receive a file describing
what's in it. You give that file to Bookhead, and Bookhead creates an enriched,
ready-to-sell product for every book in the shipment.

This is the fastest way to add many books at once and the workflow most stores
run day to day. See [Importing a distributor
file](./importing-distributor-file.md).

## 2. Manual book creation

When you only need to add a book or two — a single new arrival, a special order —
you can create products by hand. You provide a few details (the ISBN, quantity,
and price) and Bookhead fills in the cover and bibliographic data for you. See
[Creating book products manually](./creating-books-manually.md).

## 3. An external in-store sales system

If you already track inventory in a separate in-store sales system, Bookhead can
take new titles from that system as your source. Setting this up requires
coordination, so contact [support@bookhead.net](mailto:support@bookhead.net) to
arrange it.

## Shopify is not a source of new inventory

It's worth being precise about this, because it's the most common point of
confusion:

> **Bookhead does not treat your Shopify store as a source of new books.**
> New inventory always originates from one of the three sources above. Bookhead
> *reads* your Shopify catalog only to keep things in sync — to recognize books
> you already have, avoid duplicates, and update the right product when something
> changes.

For exactly how that read-and-sync behavior works, see [How syncing
works](./how-syncing-works.md).
